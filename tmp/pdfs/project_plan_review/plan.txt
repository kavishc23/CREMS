1.0 Introduction 
The Carpenters Rental & Asset Management System (CREMS) is proposed as a modular web-based 
platform for managing rentable assets across Carpenters Fiji. The approved initial implementation scope 
covers Carpenters Motors and Carptrac. The system will be made such that it would include provision to 
add future divisions and merchants such as Carpenters Shipping and Carpenters Properties. The 
common business thread is the complete asset rental and maintenance lifecycle rather than the broader 
operations of either division. 
CREMS will provide a shared control point for registering and locating assets, checking availability, 
managing customers and bookings, recording pre-hire and post-hire inspections, logging damage, 
scheduling preventive and corrective maintenance, and reporting revenue and expenditure at asset level. 
Division, branch and role boundaries will ensure users see only the work relevant to them while common 
modules can be configured for future divisions. 
The project will be delivered through twelve one-week sprints from 10 August to 30 October 2026. Week 
1 finalizes requirements and scope while establishing the shared repository and initial database 
foundation. Week 2 begins authentication, session security, and startup system services. Later sprints 
deliver organization, customer, asset, pricing, booking, inspection, maintenance and reporting modules in 
a dependency-aware sequence. The team will demonstrate increments regularly and maintain traceability 
from approved requirements to implementation and test evidence. 
 
2.0 Project Management Approach 
CREMS is delivered under an agile operating model led by the project leader, who holds overall authority 
and responsibility for managing and executing the project according to this plan and the team charter. 
The project team consists of four student developers who each take a functional lead role while retaining 
collective ownership of the codebase; members pair on higher-risk features, review one another's 
changes, and rotate meeting-note, demonstration and support duties. 
Delivery runs in weekly sprints (see Section 5) inside a twelve-week programme. Each sprint begins with 
planning and acceptance-criteria confirmation, proceeds through development and peer review

• Full accounting/ERP replacement, payroll, human resources management, and banking 
reconciliation. 
• Live telematics/GPS vendor integration, advanced route optimization, and native offline 
mobile applications. 
• Production payment gateway and third-party identity integration unless separately approved. 
• Historical data migration beyond an agreed representative pilot dataset.

9.0 Cost Management Plan 
The Cost Management Plan describes the process of estimation, budgeting, monitoring and control of 
costs in the 12-week period of delivery (10 August - 30 October 2026). As a student effort is not invoiced 
by CREMS, cost management within CREMS project is intended to be minimalistic and cash-oriented: it 
includes control of few cash expenses (hosting, email sending, domain/TLS certificate) rather than a 
comprehensive budget taking into account effort-related costs, however still following the established 
approach to cost management (basis of cost estimate, baseline, thresholds, variances, reporting) in order 
to apply it in case of a business project. 
Besides a cash-based cost management, cost management in CREMS also includes a non-cash aspect 
control of effort versus WBS to see if four-persons' limitation is maintained sprint after sprint.

10.0 Procurement Management Plan 
CREMS has minimal procurement requirements. The tech stack (React, ASP.NET Core, SQL Server 
Developer edition, Docker, GitHub, Postman and supporting libraries) is all either open-source or falls into 
free tiers or already paid for by the students/developers, so there is no procurement process required for 
the delivery of this product. 
In those cases where the purchase of an additional cash component is required, such as temporary 
hosting services or test email/domain limits. The project lead has the authority to agree to the cash ceiling 
budget stated in the Cost Baseline document. In any case where a purchase is required that goes above 
this ceiling or which provides a production service (such as hosting, payment or email/domain services), 
written permission must first be gained from the client (sponsor).

Schedule Management Plan documents how the 12 weekly sprint schedules will be developed, 
sequenced, baselined, tracked and controlled, using an Agile time-boxed approach appropriate to the 
fixed 12-week academic window and four-person team. 
12.1 Scheduling Methodology and Tools 
Element Approach

fixed-scope critical-path schedule is chosen because requirements are

tracked at the work-package/task-board level rather than as a detailed

12.2 Schedule Development 
The task list is generated straight from the Work Breakdown Structure: every WBS work package gets 
split up into sprintable tasks and assigned to a sprint which respects the corresponding technical 
dependencies. This twelve-sprint schedule becomes the schedule baseline for this plan, whose primary 
scheduling principles are as follows: 
• Sprint #1 is a combination of scope confirmation and minimal common engineering baseline 
(repository, initial database baseline), such that everything else has something to build on in the 
coming sprints. 
• Authentication and common system services immediately follow scope confirmation (Sprint #2) 
because nearly every future module requires a proven authentication layer to work properly. 
• Division/Branch/Role administration (Sprint #3) precedes the development of asset, booking, and 
inspection modules (Sprints 4–8) because those modules require proven isolation by access first. 
• Two division-specific lifecycle demonstrations (motors, Sprint #8; carptrac, Sprint #10) are 
intentionally spaced by two sprints to leverage the first division's configuration model for reuse 
and testing rather than duplication.

• The final two sprints (Sprints #11-#12) are reserved for integration and stabilization purposes and 
will not accommodate slipped functionality features.

14.0 Risk Management Plan 
Risks are reviewed weekly and after every client decision. The most likely and highest-impact risks are 
reflected in the sprint schedule so that mitigation actions are taken at the appropriate time, and risk 
owners provide status updates at team coordination meetings. 
At project closure, the project leader will analyse each risk and the risk-management process itself, 
capturing improvements as lessons learned for future group projects. 
 
14.1 Risk Management Approach 
• Risk identification is continuous: any team member, the client or the supervisor may raise a risk at 
any time; it is not limited to the weekly review. 
• Each risk is scored for Probability (1–5) and Impact (1–5) and rated using the Probability × Impact 
matrix. 
• Every risk has a single named member accountable for monitoring its triggers and executing its 
mitigation actions. Group (“All”) is used only where the risk is genuinely team wide. 
• A risk that has already occurred is closed on the register and moved to the issue log, then handled 
through the change-control process of the Project Management Plan.
